What is that picture on the left?

It shows two groups of cases, side by side, on the same horizontal line.

The top hump is the group where the answer is really no. The bottom hump is the group where the answer is really yes. The width of a hump shows how many cases sit at each score.

Left to right is the score the model gave. Low score on the left, high score on the right. The bottom hump sits further right because the model does give higher scores to the real yes cases. But the two humps overlap. That overlap is the whole problem. Some no cases get a high score and some yes cases get a low score, and no dial position can separate them cleanly.

Next

What is the dial doing?

The orange line is the dial. The rule is simple:

→ Everything to the right of the line, the model calls yes.
→ Everything to the left of the line, the model calls no.

So the dial cuts each hump into two pieces, giving four pieces in total. Those four pieces are the four counts in the box below the chart. Green means the model was right about that piece. Red means it was wrong.

Slide the dial left and the yes side grows: you catch more real cases, but you also wrongly flag more cases that were fine. Slide it right and the opposite happens. You cannot improve both sides at once. That is the trade-off every metric below is trying to measure.

AUC: the number the dial cannot change

Every metric so far depends on where you put the dial. Move it and they all move. AUC is different. It ignores the dial completely.

Here is how to read it. Pick one real yes case and one real no case at random. AUC is the chance that the model gave the yes case the higher score. It measures whether the model can put cases in the right order, not whether you chose a good cut-off.

AUC here
every dial position gives one dot on this curve

The curve is drawn by trying every dial position and plotting the result. Your current position is the orange dot. The dashed line is a model that guesses randomly.

0.5 means useless. 0.7 to 0.8 is a normal real model. Above 0.99 usually means you made a mistake and the answer leaked into the inputs.

Note: you cannot work AUC out from the four counts. Those four counts only describe one dial position. AUC needs the score of every single case.
